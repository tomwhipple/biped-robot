The four connectors on the robot

Everything plugs together with these. Only two things are bought as cables (the yellow chips say which BOM line); the rest ships attached to parts already on the order. Lengths shown are the worst-pose routed paths measured in cad/dress.py, so slack for flexing is already counted.

3-pin servo bus lead

Molex 5264 family · 2.54 mm pitch · housing ≈12×6 mm
Pinout
1 GND 2 V+ (battery) 3 DATA
Where

Every hop of both daisy chains: board → hip roll → hip pitch → knee → ankle. Each ST3215 has two internally-paralleled ports, so chaining is case-to-case with no splitters. The white shell has a friction lock — it only seats one way.

You need

8× 150 mm leads ship with the servos (6 used, 2 spare). Buy 2× ≥200 mm extensions for the hip-roll→hip-pitch hop — 170 mm at the worst pose, the one place stock leads can’t reach.  BOM item 19

XT30 — battery power

Amass XT30U · 2 mm gold bullets · shell ≈18×10 mm
Pinout
+ 11.1 V − GND
Where

The Tattu pack’s lead ends in one. It mates the pigtail, which runs through the inline power switch into the board’s screw terminal (or a DC 5.5×2.1 barrel plug — the board takes either, photo below). Rated ~15 A continuous against our ~10 A transient budget.

You need

1× pigtail + inline switch + heat-shrink, 20 AWG or thicker. The routed run is only ~30 mm, so any short pigtail works. BOM item 20

JST-SH 4-pin — IMU

STEMMA QT / Qwiic · 1.0 mm pitch · housing ≈6×4 mm
Pinout (Adafruit standard colors)
3V3 GND SDA→21 SCL→22
Where

BNO085 IMU → ESP32 I²C. The driver board has no JST-SH jack, so the cable’s other end is four male jumper pins onto the board’s GPIO header (21 SDA / 22 SCL, shared with the OLED — no address conflict).

You need

1× Adafruit 4209 (JST-SH → male jumpers, 150 mm — ample; the IMU sits on the deck beside the board). BOM item 21

Already on the board

Waveshare Servo Driver with ESP32 · 65×30 mm
Nothing to buy here

The two white ports are the same electrical bus — one per leg is purely cable routing. Power enters at the barrel/terminal block on the left edge. The USB-C is flashing and tethered debug only; operating the robot never needs it (any data-capable cable, skip if owned).

Bring-up reminder

Servos ship as ID 1 — plug them in one at a time to assign IDs 1–8 via the web UI before chaining (two same-ID servos won’t enumerate).
