# wiggle_gain.py simcheck: does `--id-wiggle` identify the body path's G? (2026-11-26, #259, #137)

Generated by `wiggle_gain.py simcheck` (CPU), from `walk_gain.py extract`'s 48 track-walk segments (11-12..16):

```bash
cd experiments/policy/x15-margin       # JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=
python wiggle_gain.py simcheck --channel roll            # ~1.5 min each, 8 workers
python wiggle_gain.py simcheck --channel yaw
python wiggle_gain.py simcheck --channel pitch
python wiggle_gain.py simcheck --channel roll --amp 0.5 --robot-path 0
```

How it is set up:
- The excitation is `track_host.py --id-wiggle`'s: the same windows, ticks and Catmull-Rom. It is added to each
  recorded walk's written goals in its double supports.
- The plant is the x1.5 probe's (`walk_gain.py`).
- The stand-in robot is the recorded robot (its body as measured: the disturbance the robot really has) plus the
  plant's response to the excitation at a known G. G is the out_gain law on the excitation.
- "true G (the law at the lines)": |1 + (G - 1) HP(f)| at the four lines.
- The reading is the instrumental-variable gain against the excitation, at lag 0. CI: resampling double supports.
- The rms column: the excitation's response against the robot's own motion, in the band.
- The session table: what a robot session of K double supports would read.
- The last table runs the `robot` command's own path on 12 of the walks, at G 1.25. That path aligns the logged
  excitation to the written goals, takes it out again and replays twice.

For comparison, the track walks themselves (walk_gain_results.md) had coherence 0.14 in roll (double support,
1-3 Hz), 0.03 or less in pitch, and 0.04-0.09 in yaw rate. Their body readings were not monotonic in G.
