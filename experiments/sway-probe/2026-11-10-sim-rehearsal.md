# 2026-11-10: sim rehearsal of the sway-probe ladder (issue #260)

What the plant predicts for each rung before the robot is asked, so every hardware rung has a sim number and
a stop criterion. Nothing here touched the robot. All numbers come from `sway_sim.py` runs on this commit, CPU
only; the JSON they came from is in `data/` and `predictions*.json`.

## 1. Setup

- Plant: `sim/bimo_biped_v6legs_meas_sole.xml` in `walker_env.BimoWalkerEnv`, built from the config.json of
  `robot_legs_prec45_ws1_only` the way the referee builds it (`eval_precision.make_env`): per-servo stiffness
  map (hip rolls x4, the rest x2.8), `act_lag_scale sts2026` (hip roll pole x4), `fw_shaper_hz 8`, integer goal
  ticks, `floor_contacts all`.
- Band: 24 Latin-hypercube plants over that recipe's own DR ranges (`sway_sim.Band`): floor friction 0.3 to 0.7,
  joint friction 0.1 to 0.4 N m, roll play 0.5 to 2.0 deg, backlash 0 to 0.25 deg, torso mass 0.6 to 1.4x, torso
  CoM +-15 mm, action-chain pole 2.65 to 8 Hz, dead time 2 to 4 ticks (STS3215) and 0 to 1 (hip rolls), latency
  0 to 8 ms, servo stiffness 0.6 to 1.2x. Seed 0, one episode per plant per rung.
- Chains: `prec` = the recipe's chain as is (policy-loop shaper 8 Hz x3 included); `pose` = the same without the
  shaper, which the bench `pose` path does not have. `pose` is the primary (it is what the sender drives);
  both are in the tables below and the conclusions agree.
- Guards, read from the firmware headers (and pinned by `tests/test_sway_probe_sim.py`): bench fall guard 30 deg
  for 100 ms (limp); the armed flail guard's tilt 20 deg / 200 ms and |gyro| EMA 1.5 rad/s (tau 0.2 s) are
  reported as margins too, although the `pose` path is bench, not armed.
- Not run: the SIL twin (`sim/sil_twin.py`). It runs the firmware's policy stack behind the UDP link and has no
  `pose` streamer, so it cannot play this open-loop ladder; the closest firmware-side check is the loopback test of
  the sender's frames against the real Wi-Fi CLI session logic (`tests/test_sway_probe_wire.py`).

## 2. The generator reproduces the training reference

`tests/test_sway_probe_ref.py`: where the envelope is 1, every keyframe equals `_mimic_ref` of a second env built
with the sway in its constructor (as `train_mjx` builds it), bit for bit, for three (M, lead) pairs; the sway rungs
are that reference's two roll channels on the standing pose. The command is a yaw command of 0.051 (just over the
reference's moving gate), which leaves 0.15 deg of stride on the hip pitch.

## 3. What the firmware can play: chained `pose s200`

`cmdPose` (`firmware/main/cli.cpp`) takes one target and 200 to 20000 ms, starting from read-back. There is no
keyframe playback. Sim of the sender's playback (`--seg-ms`) against feeding the 50 Hz keyframes straight to the
chain (`--seg-ms 0`), `pose` chain, open loop, 24 plants (median swing-foot clearance, mm; plants up of 24):

| M | kind | 50 Hz direct | s200 chained | s300 | s400 |

| 3.9 cm | sway | 3.4 (24 up) | 4.8 (24) | 5.9 (24) | 4.8 (24) |
| 5.0 cm | sway | 5.0 (24) | 7.3 (24) | 16.3 (11) | 18.6 (8) |
| 3.9 cm | lift | 19.0 (20) | 6.9 (24) | 6.9 (24) | 3.4 (24) |
| 5.0 cm | lift | 21.7 (21) | 7.3 (24) | 7.0 (24) | 4.8 (24) |

200 ms is the shortest the firmware takes and the best here. The lift rungs realize about a third of the
reference's 20 mm lift; played faithfully (direct) the reference does **not** stay up open loop on 3 to 6 of 24
plants on every lift rung up to 5.0 cm (18 to 21 up), so part of the chained playback's survival is its attenuated lift. (s300 and s400 data
for four rungs only: `data/pose_seg300_off_subset.json`, `..._seg400_...`.)

rung loses 2 (a `prec`-chain run loses none), 6.2 cm loses 11, 7.5 cm all 24. One failing plant, traced once

## 6. Alternatives

**Bench roll PI** (`--balance stance`, `sway_sim.Stream`): `pose ... s200 B<stance>` with the stance foot the one
not swinging, firmware gains (kp 0.15, ki 1.0, kd 0, clamp 10 deg, |ki I| <= 3 deg, ankle +u / hip -u, abort 12 deg),
the PI continuing across segments of the same stance and restarting when the stance changes (`cmdPose` `bal_cont`),
from `experiments/one-leg-stand/balance.RollPI`. Sway rungs: no gain (22 vs 22 up at 5.6 cm, 14 vs 13 at 6.2 cm).
Lift rungs: worse at every M (table above). It also raised the median peak tilt at the same M (5.0 cm sway:
6.7 vs 5.8 deg). Not recommended. Caveat: emulated with a perfect IMU in the sim, and with a stance that switches every half
cycle, which is not what the one-leg stands exercised.

**Trained `sway` skill** (`sway_sim.py --skill`, the referee's `hip_sway` command vy = 0.12 sin(2 pi (t-1)/1.6),
24 plants + midpoint, policy `robot_legs_prec45_ws1_only` driven in the CPU env): stays up 25/25 in both chains,
peak tilt median 8.3 deg (`prec`) and 6.7 (`pose`), torso sway amplitude 44 / 50 mm median, but the net drift over
the 6 s is 182 / 236 mm median: it side-steps, its feet do not stay planted. Weight shift happens (CoM margin median
12 to 26 mm), but there is no amplitude or lead to set, and this policy was trained without `--mimic-sway`. It is
not a sizing instrument. (CPU env, not the firmware stack.)

**Recommendation: the open-loop ladder as built, to rung 5 sway-only and rung 12 lift** (5.0 cm and 5.6 cm), with the
sender's stop criteria. If the foot has not cleared by then, the next question is the plant (#259, #82), not a
bigger open-loop amplitude.

## 7. Lead

Lead 0.785 is the training default. At the same ladder, sway-only rungs are insensitive to lead (the sway is the
same; clearance 5 mm at 3.9 cm for 0.785 and 1.2, 3 mm for 0.4). Lift rungs are not: lead 1.2 loses plants from 3.9 cm
(23, 21, 16, 8 of 24 up at 3.9, 4.5, 5.0, 5.6 cm); lead 0.4 keeps all 24 but with less CoM margin (-3 mm median at 2.0 cm,
+16 mm at 5.6 cm; `predictions_lead0.4.json`, `predictions_lead1.2.json`).

## 8. Not verified

- Everything above is sim. The roll play, torso mass and CoM, lag and the foot/floor contact are the unknowns the band spans; the robot may sit outside it.
- Dispatch: the sim starts every segment exactly on the 20 ms tick; over Wi-Fi each `pose` + `imu` pair has jitter. The sender logs each segment's lateness.
- The CPU env does not model the firmware's goal-speed register (`fw_goal_speed`).
- The roll-PI variant is an emulation of `roll_pi.h` with an ideal IMU, not the firmware.
- The stop criteria are 1.5x the sim band's worst tilt / roll rate; they have not met a real robot.
- The sender has never run `--go`; its end-to-end path is tested against a fake robot and against the loopback twin of the Wi-Fi CLI for frame acceptance, not for motion.
